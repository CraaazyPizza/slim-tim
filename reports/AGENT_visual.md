# Visual catalogue of the 119 "Engineering Infinity" manuscript scans, and a comparison against the interior symbol panel

Material: 119 scans at 5100×6600 (600 dpi), `scratchpad/ei/scans/p001.jpg … p119.jpg`.
Working copies: 1400 px wide, `scratchpad/ei/work/`. Contact sheets: `scratchpad/ei/sheets/`.
Figures made for this report: `scratchpad/ei/floor/`.

Order of work was deliberately fixed in advance: the target glyphs were characterised first,
then the manuscript was catalogued without reference to them, and only then were the two
compared. The catalogue pass was run by six observers who were **not told what the comparison
was for** and were asked only to describe marks, never to identify them.

---

## 0. Summary

- **Task A.** 119 pages catalogued. One Russian-language technical corpus in twelve numbered
  "works" (Работа № 1 … № 12), 2004–2019, on gravitational-charge dualism, fusion reactors and
  UFO propulsion. Roughly 60 % mixed typescript-plus-hand-overlay, 35 % wholly handwritten,
  5 % typed only. ~75 of 119 pages carry a figure.
- **Task B — verdict: no resemblance.** Nothing in the 119 pages resembles the symbol-panel
  glyph cluster. Detection floor measured by injection (§4): I could have detected and read the
  structure of a glyph down to ~3.7 mm on clean paper and ~7 mm on stained paper. Four
  near-misses are reported in §6 — rust offsets from a paper clip, violet list-marker slashes,
  the author's script V, and blue editorial ticks — and all four resolve to mundane causes,
  the first of them corroborated by an independent observer who was never shown the target.
- **Task C — verdict: no phrase overlap.** «сообщени*» and «предупре*» do not occur anywhere.
  «предыдущ-» occurs nine times but only in ordinary oblique-case usage ("on the previous
  page", "of previous generations"), never as the nominative «Предыдущее». No "Mark 5", no
  1961. Detection floor in §7.
- **Confidence.** Moderate-to-high on Task C (typescript, directly legible). Moderate on Task
  B: the null is well-powered against a *discrete drawn mark*, and weaker against a resemblance
  hidden inside the construction lines of a technical drawing, which I did not have a way to
  test.

## 1. What the target actually looks like

Read from `analysis/symbol-panel/glyph_01_full_cluster.png`, `glyph_02_left_crescent.png`,
`glyph_04_flag_and_bar.png` and `glyph_lineart.png` before opening any manuscript page.

The cluster is roughly square in overall aspect and made of four elements, left to right:

- **T1** a narrow closed lobe, pointed at top and bottom (a leaf or eye shape), with a straight
  stem descending from its foot and curving slightly right at the very bottom;
- **T2** a tall near-vertical stem whose top curves over to the left and ends in a point;
- **T3** a short minim starting at mid-height and descending;
- **T4** a vertical stem carrying a sharply pointed flag terminal angled up and to the left,
  **plus a horizontal bar at about 40 % of glyph height** that crosses the right-hand stem pair
  and overshoots to the right.

Diagnostic features, in decreasing order of how well this material could support them:

1. compact cluster of 3–4 separate strokes, no baseline joining them;
2. a horizontal crossbar at ~40 % height crossing a stem pair and overshooting;
3. stems **upright**, not slanted;
4. a closed pointed lobe on a straight descending stem;
5. sharply pointed, flag-like stroke terminals (calligraphic modulation).

Per `FINDINGS.md` §8 the repo's own position is that features 1–4 are supportable at the
video's resolution and stroke-terminal style (5) is not. My comparison therefore rests on 1–4.

## 3. Method, and the bias controls applied

1. **Target first, corpus second.** The symbol-panel images were characterised (§1) before any
   manuscript page was opened, so the feature list was not tuned to what the manuscript happens
   to contain.
2. **Blind describers.** Every one of the 119 pages was read individually at 1400 px by one of
   six observers, none of whom was told about the symbol panel, the video, or the purpose of
   the exercise. They were asked to *describe* marks — "strokes, curves, closures, bars,
   terminals … describe it as you would to someone who cannot see it" — and explicitly told
   **not** to identify them, and that "none" was an expected answer for most pages. The
   comparison was then made by me against their descriptions, not by them.
3. **The null offered explicitly**, in the instructions to the describers, in both clean-room
   prompts, and in this report.
4. **Unmodified frames.** All candidate re-checks were done on the 5100×6600 originals, not on
   the downsampled working copies and not on any enhanced version.
5. **Every candidate re-examined at 600 dpi by me personally** — §6 lists all of them.
6. **Detection floor measured by injection** before any absence was claimed (§4).
7. **Two clean-room second opinions with hidden positive controls** (§5).
8. Task C checked through two independent channels — image read and transcription grep (§7).

**What this design does not cover.** The describers worked at 1400 px, so their *first* pass
could not resolve stroke terminals; that is why every candidate went back to full resolution.
And injection measures sensitivity to an added mark, not to a resemblance already embedded in a
technical drawing. See §4 and §6.6.

## 4. Detection floor (measured, not asserted)

`CLAUDE.md`: never state an absence without a detection floor. I injected the target glyph,
rendered from `glyph_lineart.png` in blue ballpoint colour, into real manuscript pages **at
full resolution**, then downsampled through the identical ffmpeg path used for the working
copies, and reported where I stopped being able to read it.

**Best case — clean white paper, handwritten page (p005), blue ink at full opacity**
(`floor/inj_size_1400.png`, `floor/size_zoom.png`):

| glyph height @600 dpi | on paper | in 1400 px working copy | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 350 px | ~15 mm | 96 px | all four elements legible, crossbar obvious |
| 175 px | ~7.4 mm | 48 px | all four elements legible, crossbar clear |
| 88 px | ~3.7 mm | 24 px | cluster recognisable, crossbar discernible — **structural floor** |
| 44 px | ~1.9 mm | 12 px | a mark is visible; structure not recoverable — **presence floor** |
| 22 px | ~0.9 mm | 6 px | not detectable against the text |

**Worst case — yellowed paper, over dense typescript (p078)** (`floor/inj_worst_1400.png`):
the structural floor degrades from ~3.7 mm to **~7 mm**; the 3.7 mm instance is visible as a
blue cluster but its internal structure is marginal.

**Contrast**, at 5 mm size on clean paper (`floor/inj_contrast_1400.png`): still detectable at
**10 % of full ink opacity**. This was measured in a blank margin, which is the easy case; I
did **not** measure the contrast floor over dense text and do not claim one.

**So, concretely, what I could and could not have detected.**

- **Could** detect and structurally read: any discrete drawn mark ≥ ~4 mm on clean paper or
  ≥ ~7 mm on stained paper — i.e. anything at or above the size of the author's own capital
  letters. Every deliberate symbol, label or device in this corpus is at or above that size.
- **Could** detect the presence of, without reading: marks down to ~2 mm.
- **Could not** detect: marks below ~1 mm; stroke-terminal style, pen-pressure modulation and
  ink-width variation at any size in the 1400 px copies — for those I went back to the
  5100×6600 originals, and did so for every candidate in §6.
- **Could not** test: whether some sub-part of a technical drawing (a coil, a brace, a vector
  cluster) constitutes a resemblance. Injection measures sensitivity to an *added* mark; it
  says nothing about a resemblance that is already an intended part of a diagram. This is the
  main residual weakness of the Task B null and I flag it rather than paper over it.

**Independent check on the floor.** Two clean-room runs (§5) with a hidden positive control
both found the injected glyph — including on the yellowed, text-dense page and at a coarser
1100 px page scale than my own working copies. So the floor above is, if anything,
conservative.

## 5. Second opinions, in a clean room

Run per `CLAUDE.md`: neutral filenames, outside the repo, `-p` supplied, environment stripped.
Neither run was told what the material was, what the reference shape was, or what answer was
expected. Each contained a **hidden positive control** so that a null could be distinguished
from a blind observer.

**Run 1 — region discrimination** (`/tmp/vq2`). Reference = target glyph. Seven candidates:
six genuine manuscript regions (p008, p041, p071, p090, p117, p031) and one injected page.
Result: the positive control was found; **all six genuine regions returned "no resemblance"**.

**Run 2 — whole-page sweep** (`/tmp/vq3`). Reference = target glyph. Eight full pages at
1100 px: seven genuine (p001, p012, p024, p056, p074, p099, p111) and one injected. Result:
the control was found *and correctly localised to three instances*; **all seven genuine pages
returned "no resemblance"**.

**Run 3 — the near-miss put to an independent observer** (`/tmp/vq4`). The p049, p055 and p100
top-left marks were shown with no reference shape and no leading question — only "what do you
see, what caused these markings, say so if nothing is identifiable". Unprompted, the observer
returned **"corroded metal paper clip"** for p049 and p055, and "a blue pen check mark" for
p100. That is an independent arrival at the same explanation I had reached from the montage,
and it was reached without the target glyph ever being shown.

## 6. Task B — the comparison, and the near-misses

**Verdict: no mark, glyph, logo, symbol or diagram element in the 119 pages resembles the
symbol-panel cluster.** The null is on the table and this is the answer the evidence gives.

The corpus contains, across 119 pages: Cyrillic Russian in typescript and cursive; Latin and
Greek letters as physics symbols; digits; standard engineering-drawing apparatus (hatching,
braces, dimension arrows, coils, field lines, section marks); footnote asterisks; hand-ruled
tables and plots. **No stamps, seals, letterheads, logos, emblems, insignia, monograms or
signature devices occur anywhere.** No writing in any script other than Cyrillic, Latin and
Greek occurs anywhere. The only Latin-script *words* in 119 pages are "USA" (p019), "Spin"
(p092), "Const", and catalogue designations NGC 4565 / NGC 1300 / M51.

Three near-misses deserve to be recorded, because each is the kind of thing that would have
been reported as a match by a less careful pass. All three were re-examined at full 600 dpi.

### 6.1 The rust offsets at the top-left corner (strongest near-miss)

About a dozen sheets — p034, p047, p049, p051, p052, p054, p055, p078, p084, p090, p096, p099,
p100 — carry a rust-brown mark complex at the same position near the top-left corner. Asked to
describe these neutrally, one observer produced: *"a crescent open at the top … a near-vertical
line leaning right … a bold solid stroke … a mark shaped like a flag: a short horizontal bar at
the top, from whose right end a stroke descends"*. That is, in the abstract, a fair paraphrase
of the target's element list, and it is **the single closest thing in the corpus** to the
symbol-panel cluster.

It is not a match, and the reasons are decisive:

- At 600 dpi (`floor/p049_tl.png`) the mark resolves into **the silhouette of a paper clip** —
  a rounded-rectangular outer loop with a shorter inner loop, traced in translucent orange,
  with darker rust deposits where the wire bore on the sheet.
- Laid side by side across the affected sheets (`floor/stains.png`), the clip outline is
  directly visible on p055, p084, p099, p100 and p034. The "vertical strokes with a hook" are
  the clip's two nested loops.
- The form **varies from sheet to sheet**. A repeated designed mark would be reproducible;
  stochastic corrosion is not. This is the discriminating test and it comes out against a glyph.
- The colour — rust orange grading to pale yellow, diffusing into the paper with a halo —
  matches no ink used anywhere in the corpus (black, blue, cyan, red, green, violet).
- An independent observer, shown these with no reference shape and no leading question,
  said "corroded metal paper clip" (§5, run 3).

### 6.2 The violet slashes on p020

Four violet-ink marks, each two or three parallel oblique strokes, one after each enumerator
а), б), в), Г). Violet occurs nowhere else in the corpus, which is what drew attention to them.
At full resolution (`floor/p020_a.png`) they are plainly **list-marker slashes ("//")**. They
share exactly one feature with the target — "parallel strokes" — which is the least diagnostic
feature on the list. They have no crossbar, no loop, no hook, no flag, blunt terminals, and
they lean ~60° where the target's stems are upright.

### 6.3 The bold script V on p021

Reported by an observer as an unidentified heavy black glyph with a loop and a rising terminal.
At full resolution (`floor/p021_a.png`) it is the author's **calligraphic capital script V**,
his symbol for velocity, used three times on that page in the expressions 𝒱{Vμμ̃}, 𝒱{ℬ} and
𝒱{Ve⁺e⁻}. A single letter, not a cluster; no crossbar.

Also checked and resolved: the compound glyph at the top right of p045, which an observer could
not read confidently, is an over-inked handwritten **"12"** (`floor/p045_tr.png`) — consistent
with the folio sequence p044 = 11, p046 = 13. The recurring "L-shaped device" beside folio
numbers on p004–p010, p046, p049, p050, p085 and p096 is a hand-drawn corner bracket around the
page number.

### 6.4 The blue ticks in the top margin

Blue two-stroke marks recur in the top margin of p100, p101, p102, p103 and p107, described by
one observer as "a narrow open V" and "a narrow U with a rounded bottom", and noted as running
off the trimmed sheet edge on p101 and p103. At 600 dpi (`floor/bluev.png`) they are ordinary
**editorial check marks** — a short left stroke and a longer right stroke meeting at a low
vertex where the ink pools. The independent observer, shown p100 with no reference shape,
independently called it "a blue pen check mark" (§5, run 3). Two strokes, no crossbar, no loop,
no third element.

### 6.5 Devices checked and found to be ordinary drafting apparatus

- The "equal-armed cross with T-terminals" on **p116** and the "eight-armed cross" on **p107**
  are axis/dimension crosses with tick terminators — p107's carries the digits 1–8 because it
  *is* the author's eight-point "evolutionary circle" redrawn as axes (`floor/p116_x.png`).
- The L-shaped "device" recurring beside folio numbers on p004–p010, p046, p049, p050, p085 and
  p096 is a hand-drawn corner bracket around the page number.
- The over-inked glyph at the top right of **p045** is a handwritten **"12"**
  (`floor/p045_tr.png`), consistent with the folio sequence p044 = 11, p046 = 13.
- The green "stroke bundles with serrated flag-like fringes" on p029, p031, p032, p117 and p118
  are field-line bundles with arrowheads, part of the diagrams they sit in.
- The repeated "solid rosette / star" glyphs on p038, p039, p054, p091, p092 and p104 are
  typewriter and pen **asterisks used as footnote keys**.
- The apparent "photographic insets" on p058–p060 are hand cross-hatched magnet blocks labelled
  N and S, not pasted photographs (`floor/p058_ins.png`).

### 6.6 The honest structural caveat

Russian orthography inherently supplies the target's *primitives*. «Н» is two stems joined by a
mid-height crossbar; «Ш» and «Щ» are minim clusters; «Ф» is a closed lobe on a vertical stem.
Every page of this manuscript therefore contains sub-elements that, taken in isolation, resemble
parts of the panel glyph. What no page contains is the specific **combination** at the specific
proportions: a bar at 40 % height crossing only the right stem pair and overshooting it, no
baseline join, a pointed flag terminal angled up-left, and a closed pointed lobe on a straight
descending tail — with all stems upright.

The author's hand is additionally a fluent, connected cursive with a consistent 15–20° rightward
slant (`floor/hand_letters.png`, `floor/hand_letters2.png`). The target's strokes are upright and
separate. That is a systematic difference, not a stylistic quibble, and it holds across all 35-odd
handwritten pages.

## 7. Task C — the Cyrillic captions

**Verdict: no phrase, header or caption in these 119 pages overlaps with the video captions.**

**Image side.** Six observers read all 119 pages and searched headers, captions, figure labels,
footnotes, folio areas and marginalia for the roots «предыдущ», «сообщен(и)», «предупре(жда)»,
the standalone token «АА», the string "Mark 5" and the year 1961.

**Text side.** The bilingual critical editions in `scratchpad/ei/txt/` (Works 01–12, ~822 kB,

| «Предыдущее сообщени[е]» | **«сообщен-» does not occur at all**, on either side of the check. «предыдущ-» does occur, but always in an oblique case and always in one of two ordinary senses: «на предыдущей странице» ("on the previous page" — located on **p042, p060, p061**, and twice in the Work 03 transcription) and «предыдущих поколений / предыдущих Усопших Поколений» ("of previous generations" — **p104, p105**, and four times in the Work 10 transcription). **Detection floor for Task C.** What a hit would have looked like, and what I could and could
not have caught:

- **Could** catch reliably: any occurrence in the typescript. The typed body text is clean,
  monospace or proportional serif, ~3 mm cap height, and directly legible in the 1400 px copies;
  and it is independently corroborated by the transcription corpus, which is a separate
  channel from the image read. A typed «сообщение» anywhere would have been caught twice over.
- **Could** catch: any occurrence in a heading, folio line, figure caption or boxed note — these
  are written larger than the body text throughout.
- **Weaker**: the densest blue-ink cursive marginalia (noted specifically on p062's rotated left
  margin, p064's lower-left block, p065 and p070's caption clusters). Individual words there are
  legible, but a short root could in principle hide inside a cramped ligature. I am not claiming
  the same strength for those regions as for the typescript.
- **Could not** catch: anything on a verso, on a sheet not in this set of 119, or in the parts
  of p040 and p097 trimmed off by the scan.
- One structural point worth stating plainly: the two video captions and this manuscript are
  different registers. The captions are a *narrative address to a viewer*; the manuscript is a
  technical argument in the first person about physics. The vocabulary of the former
  (сообщение, предупреждать) has no occasion to appear in the latter, so the null here is
  unsurprising rather than informative — it constrains very little either way.

**A caveat on the text-side channel.** The files in `txt/` are "critical editions", i.e. edited
derivatives, not diplomatic transcriptions of these 119 scans, and their page coverage is not
guaranteed to be 1:1. They corroborate the image read; they do not replace it. The image read
is the primary evidence for Task C.

## 9. Privacy

No page carries a postal address, telephone number, email address or ID number.

Personal names occur only as (a) scientific eponyms in ordinary technical prose — Einstein,
Newton, Coulomb, Mössbauer, Pauli, Rutherford, Bohr, Michelson, Morley, Doppler, Laplace,
Curie, Laval, Mendeleev, Kant; and (b) cited published authors in bibliographic references, on
**p015, p063, p065, p099 and p111**; and (c) public historical and political figures in the
typed dated lists on **p075** and as intellectual references on **p104, p107 and p116**. Values
of (b) and (c) are not transcribed here.

Per `CLAUDE.md`, this was built by inclusion: the privacy sweep was a page-by-page enumeration
by observers instructed to flag rather than transcribe, not a text grep — which would have
missed names rendered inside images, and the bibliographic citations on p063, p065, p099 and
p111 are typed *into* the scans, not into any text layer.

The manuscript is **unsigned throughout**. No signature, monogram, dedication, letterhead or
marginal personal note appears on any of the 119 pages.
