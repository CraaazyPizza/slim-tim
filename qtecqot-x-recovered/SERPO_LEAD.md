That last line is the whole reason this file exists.

## SERPO Release 26a: "RETIRED KGB MAJOR IVAN WRITES"

Project SERPO is a documented UFO-lore corpus released from **2005-11-02** onward at
`serpo.org` by a poster calling himself "Anonymous", claiming a 1965–78 exchange programme with
a planet of Zeta Reticuli. Its material is published as numbered **Releases**. The domain is now
parked and answers 403; the Wayback Machine holds `serpo.org` from 2005-12-08, and the full text
survives in mirrors.

Two of those releases are Chapter 9, "Soviet Contact":

| | |
|---|---|
| **Release 26** | "A violent contact with ETs in Russia" — the *American version*, a CIA-sourced account |
| **Release 26a** | "Russian Version of Violent ET Encounter from KGB" — headed **`RETIRED KGB MAJOR IVAN WRITES:`** |

Release 26a is a first-person account, attributed to a retired KGB major named **Ivan**, of an
11 January 1985 incident: two objects penetrate Soviet airspace over Siberia, one lands at a
missile base near Zhmurova, guards fire on it, and **five non-human occupants are taken alive**
into Soviet military custody at Irkutsk and then south of Moscow. It describes them:

> "The occupants were described as being approximately 1.2 meters in height and weighed
> approximately 100 kilograms. The occupants had no ears, no thumbs, small horizontal openings
> for mouths and no hair. The occupants were wearing identical one-piece flying suits, gray in
> colour."

> "The doctor told the commander in charge that THE INJURED OCCUPANT WAS NOT HUMAN. The injured
> occupant's blood was thick white fluid."

> "Several interrogation sessions occurred with the occupants by KGB and Soviet Military
> Linguists from the SECOND Chief Directorate … but the occupants never spoke nor communicated."

And in the American half, Release 26, the line that matters most here:

> "CAC reported Soviet KGB officials took over 100 PHOTOGRAPHS and **one (1) video recording of
> the occupants**. CAC provided 25 copies of the photographs to this [CIA] CO."

**A single KGB video recording of live non-human occupants in Soviet custody, described in a
published text in 2006 — five years before ivan0135 uploaded one in May 2011.**

Source: full text mirrored at
`https://www.oocities.org/maurice_osborn/Serpo.htm` (local copy of the fetch and the extraction
script under the session scratchpad; re-fetchable, the mirror is static). The index in that same
document lists "RELEASE 26 A violent contact with ETs in Russia" and "RELEASE 26a Russian
Version of Violent ET Encounter from KGB" as discrete releases, so the numbering is the corpus's
own, not a mirror's invention.

## What does not line up

Recording the misses matters as much, and they are real:

- **Date.** Release 26a is a January **1985** incident. The Skinny Bob corpus is dated
  **1942–1969**. They are not the same event, and no reading makes them one.
- **Build.** 1.2 m and 100 kg is squat and heavy. "Skinny Bob" is not.
- **Anatomy.** "No thumbs" is not what the 2011 footage shows.
- **Ending.** In 26a all five occupants escape on 14 Jan 85 and the craft flies out of its
  hangar. Nothing in the Skinny Bob corpus corresponds.
- **The word "Ivan"** is among the most common Russian given names, and Release 26a gives no
  surname. On its own the name is weak; it is the *stack* — Ivan **plus** KGB **plus** a single
  video recording of live occupants **plus** the word СЕРПО written by qtecqot himself — that
  carries the weight.

## What this supports, and what it does not

**Supported.** The operator of the qtecqot account explicitly named Project SERPO in the first
thing he ever posted, and then deleted it. SERPO's Chapter 9 contains a named "KGB Major Ivan"
and a stated single KGB video recording of live non-human occupants, published years before
ivan0135 existed. There is a documented prior text that the 2011 persona's two defining
features — the name and the premise — are both already present in.

**Not supported.** That the 2011 uploader read it, that the footage derives from it, or anything
about how the footage was made. A shared genre ancestor is not a production credit. Everything in
`FINDINGS.md` about how the videos were assembled stands or falls on its own evidence and is
untouched by this.

**Worth stating plainly:** this cuts against the "qtecqot is an unrelated 2026 impostor" reading
and against the "qtecqot is a naive AI-video account" reading alike. Whoever runs it knew the
lore's primary sources well enough to cite the right release series in Russian, in his first
post, before any video shipped — and then judged it a liability and removed it. Same editorial
reflex, same week, as the Černohajev deletion.
